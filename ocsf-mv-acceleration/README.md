# ocsf-mv-acceleration: what a materialized view buys a SOC dashboard, and what it costs

## What it measures

`run.py` (R5) ingests 20,000,000 OCSF events in 20 batches and serves three dashboard panels two ways: a base-table scan on every refresh, and a small additive materialized view kept current by merging each batch's partial aggregate. It records read latency for both, per-batch maintenance cost against a full recompute, and the view's storage overhead, in DuckDB. `crossover.py` (T2.4) then sweeps the base size at a fixed 200,000-row batch to find where incremental maintenance overtakes a full recompute, for a bounded-cardinality view (user name by destination port) and an unbounded one (source IP).

## Result, and whether it is measured

Measured, with answers checked: every panel's view returns the same rows as its base query (`answers_agree: true`, `results/results.json`). Read speedups are 76.8× (class rollup), 53.6× (5-minute time series) and 45.3× (failed auth by user). Incremental maintenance is 2.9× cheaper than recompute on the 12-row class rollup and about even on the 2,000-row failed-auth view. The crossover sweep finds incremental maintenance first winning at a base-to-batch ratio of about 25:1 for the bounded view and never winning in the swept range for the unbounded one, whose view grows almost as large as the base (`results/crossover.json`). A large read speedup is expected from a view of 12 to 2,000 rows in place of 20 million, so the more informative results are the maintenance and crossover numbers.

## What would have made it fail

The base scan is the baseline for every speedup, and full recompute is the baseline for maintenance; incremental maintenance did lose to recompute on the unbounded view, so the comparison could and did go the other way. The 76.8× rests on a base scan with a CV of 39.5%, above the 30% ceiling other benches in this lab use; its gap is far larger than that CV, but the figure is less settled than the other two (CVs 7.2% and 11.2%).

## Pre-registration

None.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 ocsf-mv-acceleration/run.py         # default 20M events, 20 batches; writes results/results.json and results/RESULTS.md
python3 ocsf-mv-acceleration/crossover.py   # base:batch sweep; writes results/crossover.json and results/CROSSOVER.md
```

`run.py` imports the corpus generator from `../clickhouse-vs-duckdb/` and helpers from `../lib/`.

## Results

- Entry point: `results/RESULTS.md` (R5), regenerated by `run.py`.
- `results/CROSSOVER.md` (T2.4), regenerated by `crossover.py`.
- Data: `results/results.json`, `results/crossover.json`.

## Versions and pins

DuckDB, from the root `requirements.txt` (`duckdb==1.5.3`); the result files do not record the version they ran on.

## Evidence tier and limits

Tier B, one host, synthetic events, additive count and sum panels only. A view answers only the questions decided in advance, and ad-hoc queries still pay the base scan.
